Part I — The brief
01

The problem statement

Detect abnormal, inconsistent or faulty observations from Automatic Weather Stations in real time, using only Temperature (°C), Atmospheric Pressure (hPa) and Relative Humidity (%). Distinguish genuine meteorological events from sensor/data anomalies while minimising false alarms and enabling scalable deployment across large networks.

RequiredDetail
ObjectivesReal-time detection · identify sensor faults, spikes, frozen values, communication errors · learn normal temporal and seasonal patterns · multivariate consistency analysis · confidence scores and explainable reasoning · predict sensor degradation and maintenance needs · optionally suggest corrected values.
OutputsReal-time alerts · severity and confidence scores · root-cause classification · visualisation dashboard · sensor health status · corrected data (optional).
Deliverable"Fully executable code with example usage and a document explaining various use cases." Not a prototype and slides.
Evaluated onAnomaly-injected data.

The one case that matters

The hard failure is drift. A probe that fails slowly reads a little wrong, then a little more, for weeks. No single reading is implausible, so no threshold fires. The failure is not the value — it is the divergence, and divergence is only visible against something that has not diverged. That is the idea the whole system rests on: compare a station with its neighbours at the same moment. Weather is shared between nearby stations and cancels in the difference; a fault belongs to one instrument and does not.

Consequence

A station with fewer than three usable neighbours cannot be judged this way. The interface labels those NO NEIGHBOURS rather than guessing — visible on the Andaman & Nicobar stations in the station index.

Part II — The mathematics
04

The physics you have

Three measured numbers. Everything else is derived from them by thermodynamics, and the derived quantities are more useful than the raw ones because they behave differently under weather than under failure.

Saturation vapour pressure — the Magnus form

Air can hold only so much water vapour before it condenses, and that ceiling rises steeply with temperature. The Magnus formula gives that ceiling:

es(T) = 6.112 · exp( a·T / (b + T) ) hPa a = 17.62, b = 243.12 over water, T ≥ 0 °C a = 22.46, b = 272.62 over ice, T < 0 °C

The two coefficient sets matter. Using the water set below freezing manufactures spurious violations at high-altitude and northern stations — the formula would claim the air is supersaturated when it is simply cold.

Actual vapour pressure and dew point

Relative humidity is a percentage of that ceiling, which is why RH alone tells you little: 60% at 30 °C is far more water than 60% at 5 °C. Multiply to get the actual amount, then invert to get the temperature at which that amount would saturate — the dew point.

e = (RH / 100) · es(T) hPa γ = ln(RH/100) + a·T/(b+T) Td = b·γ / (a − γ) °C
Worked — T = 30 °C, RH = 60 %, P = 1000 hPa
es = 6.112 · exp(17.62·30 / (243.12+30))42.3372 hPa
e = 0.60 · 42.337225.4023 hPa
γ = ln(0.60) + 17.62·30/273.121.4245
Td = 243.12·1.4245 / (17.62 − 1.4245)21.39 °C
Dew point21.39 °C

Sanity check by eye: the air is at 30 °C and would need to cool to about 21 °C before dew forms. That is a humid but ordinary tropical afternoon. If the formula had returned 35 °C — a dew point above the air temperature — that would mean RH above 100%, which is the same statement, not a second independent check.

Specific humidity — this is what pressure is for

Specific humidity is the mass of water vapour per mass of air, in g/kg. Unlike RH, it does not change when the air simply warms or cools.

q = 0.622 · e / (P − 0.378·e) · 1000 g/kg
Worked — same air parcel
0.622 · 25.402315.8002
P − 0.378·e = 1000 − 9.602990.398
q = 15.8002 / 990.398 · 100015.95 g/kg
Specific humidity15.95 g/kg

Why this is the useful variable: q is conserved under dry adiabatic processes and under ordinary diurnal heating. The sun warms the air, RH falls, and q does not move. So an RH sensor that drifts breaks q, whereas genuine advection — a moist air mass arriving — moves q coherently and moves it at the neighbours too. That is a physical discriminator between fault and weather, available from exactly the three parameters the problem statement allows.

Pressure must be reduced to sea level before comparison

Pressure falls roughly 1 hPa per 8–9 m of altitude. Comparing raw station pressure between neighbours at different elevations is meaningless.

PMSL = Pstation · exp( h / (29.271 · (T + 273.15)) ) h in metres
Worked — two neighbours, 950 hPa each, T = 25 °C
Station A at 500 m1006.02 hPa
Station B at 600 m1017.61 hPa
Apparent difference if reduction is skipped11.59 hPa

Eleven and a half hectopascals of pure artefact from 100 m of hill, against a genuine synoptic pressure anomaly that is often under 5 hPa. Skip this step and every hill station looks broken.

05

Why three parameters are enough

The constraint looks severe until you notice that T, P and RH are not three independent numbers — they are three views of one thermodynamic state, tied together by the relations above. That gives two independent kinds of evidence:

EvidenceQuestion it answersCatches
Cross-channel coherenceDo T, P and RH tell a consistent story at this station?A single failed channel: RH drifts while T and P do not, so q breaks while the neighbours' q holds.
Spatial coherenceDoes this station agree with its neighbours at this moment?Anything that moves one instrument and not the weather — drift, offset, freeze, spike.

The discriminator between a fault and a storm is not magnitude. A genuine squall line produces a large, fast pressure fall — and so does a failing barometer. What separates them is that the squall arrives at the neighbours too, a few minutes earlier or later, and keeps T, RH and P mutually consistent while it does. The fault does neither. Magnitude is not the signal; coherence is.

06

Step 1 — anomaly, before anything else

The naive method is to subtract a neighbour's reading from this station's reading. Measured on this network, that flagged 49% of healthy stations.

The reason is that a station's reading contains its own permanent identity. A mast at 4,196 m is colder than neighbours at 200 m, and — crucially — its diurnal shape differs too, because thin air warms and cools faster. So the raw difference swings every single day for reasons that have nothing to do with any instrument.

The fix is to remove each station's own clean-period climatology by hour of day first:

# hour of day for every step h(t) = (t · 15 min / 60) mod 24 # mean of each hour over the clean period, per station s clim[h, s] = mean{ x(t, s) : t < clean_steps, h(t) = h } anomaly a(t, s) = x(t, s) − clim[h(t), s]

Now every station's anomaly is centred near zero regardless of altitude, and shares the same units and roughly the same daily shape. What remains in a is weather plus faults — which is exactly what the difference is meant to compare.

07

Step 2 — difference against the neighbours

For each station, take the median anomaly of its neighbours at the same timestep and subtract it:

r(t, s) = a(t, s) − median{ a(t, j) : j ∈ N(s) } N(s) = neighbours of s; requires |N(s)| ≥ 3, else the station is not scored

Median, not mean, on purpose: the median of five neighbours is unmoved by one of them being broken, while the mean is dragged by it. This is the cheapest robustness in the whole pipeline.

Worked — one timestep, temperature anomalies in °C
Neighbour anomalies+0.80 · +0.92 · +0.71 · +1.08 · +0.85
Their median+0.85
Healthy station reads+0.90
residual r = 0.90 − 0.85+0.05
Same station with a +2.0 °C offset fault+2.90
residual r = 2.90 − 0.85+2.05

Read the healthy row carefully: every station in the region is about 0.85 °C warmer than its own climatology — a genuine warm day. The weather cancelled completely, leaving 0.05. The faulty row keeps almost the entire 2.0 °C of the fault. That contrast is the entire method.

A second, independent question: did the region move at all?

The residual above says this station is out of step with its neighbours. It says nothing about whether the neighbours were themselves doing something unusual at the time — and that is the difference between a drifting instrument in still conditions and a real front that this station merely exaggerated.

So a separate quantity is computed over the flagged window: the neighbour median anomaly, standardised by its own quiet spread. It is not what the grade was computed from, which is what makes it evidence rather than a restatement.

# what the region itself did, over the flagged window [lo, hi) region(t) = median{ a(t, j) : j ∈ N(s) } σregion = 1.4826 · median( |region − median(region)| ) whole record zregion = median( region[lo:hi] ) / σregion # agreement needs all three, not just the first moved = |zregion| ≥ 2.0 same way = sign(region_dev) = sign(station_dev) explains = |region_dev| ≥ 0.4 · |station_dev|

The third clause is the one that matters. A region that moved 2σ while the station moved 30 does not excuse the station — that is weather plus a fault, and the fault is the part worth dispatching for.

What this replaced, and why it was wrong

The board previously asserted neighbours_agree: false on every simulated row, reasoning that a flagged station is by definition one its neighbours disagree with. That handed the context agent its own conclusion: it could never veto, and its exact Shapley value across the whole board was structurally zero — three agents, one of which could not affect any outcome.

Measured against the injected truth — all 60 flagged rows
Vetoes issued (“this is weather”)9
Correct — no fault had been injected8

Real faults wrongly vetoed1

The single miss is a dropout, and the adjudicator alarms on it anyway because hardware is checked before the weather veto — a dead link is a fault in any weather. That ordering was chosen for a different reason and happens to cover this case, which is worth knowing rather than claiming as design.

08

Step 3 — how big is "big"? Sigma from the MAD

A residual of 2.05 °C means nothing until you know the ordinary spread of that station's residuals. A calm inland site might sit within ±0.2 °C; a coastal site in monsoon might routinely swing ±1.5 °C. So each station gets its own scale, estimated robustly.

What the MAD is, and where 1.4826 comes from

The Median Absolute Deviation is the median of the distances from the median. Unlike the standard deviation it is not inflated by a handful of extreme values — which matters enormously here, because the extreme values are the faults we are trying to measure around.

m = median(r) MAD = median( |r − m| ) σ = 1.4826 · MAD

The constant is not arbitrary. For data that really is Gaussian, the MAD comes out at about 0.6745σ — that is the 75th percentile of the standard normal. Dividing by 0.6745 gives 1.4826, so the scaled MAD is directly comparable to a standard deviation. You can check it: 1 / 0.6745 = 1.4826.

Worked — ten residuals, °C
r0.12 −0.05 0.31 −0.22 0.08 0.44 −0.11 0.02 0.19 −0.30
median m0.050
|r − m|0.07 0.10 0.26 0.27 0.03 0.39 0.16 0.03 0.14 0.35
median of those = MAD0.150
σ = 1.4826 · 0.1500.2224 °C

Shrinkage toward the network

One window of one station is a noisy estimate, and the stations it underestimates are exactly the ones that will cry wolf. So each station's own estimate is pulled halfway toward the network-wide median of all stations' MADs:

pooled = medianall stations( MAD ) sd = max( 0.5·MAD + 0.5·pooled , 1e−6 )
Worked — continuing, with a network pooled MAD of 0.42
own estimate0.2224
pooled0.4200
sd = 0.5·0.2224 + 0.5·0.42000.3212 °C

The window is trailing, lagged, and re-estimated on a stride

This is the correction that mattered most in the whole project.

ParameterValueWhy
SIGMA_WIN15 daysLong enough for four independent samples given a 2.5-day weather correlation time
SIGMA_LAG1 dayHolds the reference behind the point being scored, so a slow drift cannot hide inside its own sigma
SIGMA_STRIDE6 hoursThe spread of a weather residual does not move in six hours; exact rolling MAD buys nothing for the cost

The bug this replaced — measured

Sigma used to be the MAD of the first half of the record, applied to all of it. On a network with no faults injected at all:

baseline window  |z| p95 = 2.78
scoring period   |z| p95 = 4.47  ← inflated 1.61×
false alarms at 4σ: 7.6% of station-hours

Two mistakes, both the same mistake. The estimate was in-sample — fitted to the window it was then evaluated on — and it was fixed, so a month of changing weather was scored against a fortnight of one regime. A threshold of 4 was really about 2.5 by the end, and that is the entire false-alarm rate. It is also the only version a deployed system could run: at 09:00 you have yesterday, not next week.

Self-masking is the reason for the one-day lag. A drift that has already entered the reference window inflates sigma and thereby hides itself. Holding the reference a day behind buys the detector the beginning of the fault before the fault can start defending itself. This does not solve self-masking for a fault longer than the window — nothing that uses the station's own history can — and that limit is stated wherever the recall figure appears.

09

Step 4 — the z-score and the two bands

z(t, s) = ( r(t, s) − m ) / sd |z| ≥ 6 → WATCH WATCH_SIGMA = 6.0 |z| ≥ 8 → FAULT FAULT_SIGMA = 8.0
Worked — carrying both stations through, sd = 0.3212, m = 0.050
Healthy: z = (0.050 − 0.050) / 0.32120.00 → ok
Offset fault: z = (2.050 − 0.050) / 0.32126.23 → WATCH

Six sigma sounds extreme against the usual "3 sigma" intuition, and it is deliberately so. Three sigma on a Gaussian is roughly one exceedance in 370 samples; across 344 stations at 96 steps a day, that is around 90 false flags per day from noise alone. Residual distributions also have heavier tails than a Gaussian, which makes it worse. The bands were calibrated empirically against a fault-free control run rather than assumed from theory, which is why they are 6 and 8 rather than 3.

Both constants are named once, in simulate/faults_and_export.py, and imported everywhere else. They were previously written in two places, and the map and the alert list disagreed about what was flagged.

10

Two passes — a faulty neighbour poisons the reference

Measured on the first version: healthy stations had |z| p95 = 2.00 in the training window — exactly what the MAD predicts — and 4.33 afterwards, which flagged half of them. The residual was not drifting. The reference was.

A frozen or stepped station still sits inside its neighbours' median, and where a station has only four or five neighbours, one bad member moves that median enough to condemn the rest.

pass 1 z₁ = grade(all stations) suspect if a station is beyond 3σ for more than 20% of the record suspect(s) = meant( maxchannels |z₁(t,s)| ≥ 3 ) > 0.20 pass 2 z = grade(all stations, excluding suspects from everyone's N(s))

Note what is not consulted: ground truth. The second pass uses only the first pass's own output, which is what a deployed system would have.

11

What the detector can and cannot see

The bands and sigma together imply a hard detection floor, and it is worth stating in physical units rather than leaving it implicit.

A drift must accumulate past 6·sd before it reaches WATCH. With the worked example's sd of 0.3212 °C:

Drift rateReaches WATCH (6σ = 1.927 °C)Reaches FAULT (8σ = 2.570 °C)
0.15 °C / day12.8 days17.1 days
0.05 °C / day38.5 days51.4 days
0.0642 °C / day30 days — the whole recordnever, in this record
Say this out loud

Against a 30-day record and this sigma, a drift slower than roughly 0.064 °C/day cannot reach the WATCH band at all, and slower than 0.086 °C/day cannot reach FAULT. It is not that the detector is tuned badly — the evidence is not there yet. A longer record moves this floor down proportionally.

This is also the honest answer to "why is recall 57% and not 95%": the injected drift amplitudes deliberately go down to 0.005 °C/day, far below anything a month of data can support. A detector reporting 99% recall on this set would be reporting a bug.

The second blind spot is dropout. A station that stops reporting has no value to difference, so it produces no residual and no z at all. It is NO DATA, not a fault — which is why the results carry a second recall figure with dropouts excluded.

12

Alerts as episodes, not flags

A grade is recomputed every step with no memory of the last one. Thresholding it directly produced 2,484 flagged runs, half of them a single step long. An alert therefore has a beginning and an end.

Part III — The data
13

The network we made

The generator places stations at real IMD coordinates and synthesises a physically coherent weather field over them, then injects faults into a known subset. Because the truth is known by construction, recall and precision can be computed exactly — which is impossible with real IMD data, since no labelled fault list is published.

Station assignment to states is by point-in-polygon against a simplified boundary, with a nearest-polygon fallback for coastal and island sites that fall just outside their polygon. The fallback ranks by state, not by polygon — ranking by polygon made an island's runner-up the same state and the ambiguity test always passed.

Fault models — exactly what is injected

Seven fault types, each with an amplitude range and duration drawn log-uniformly. Amplitudes deliberately span from trivially detectable down to physically impossible to detect in this record, so that the result is a detection curve rather than a single flattering number.

Durations run from 1–3 hours for a spike to 240–1440 hours for a calibration drift. Weights follow real AWS failure logs, where drift and frozen values dominate.

Why the ranges go down so low

"A detector tuned against 5 K spikes will report 99% recall and miss every real 0.4 K drift." The point of the low end is to make the evaluation produce a probability-of-detection versus amplitude curve, which tells you the smallest fault the system can actually find — the number an operator needs — instead of one recall figure that hides it.

One subtlety worth knowing: for spikes, only the displaced samples are marked as touched. Marking the whole window depressed every spike metric in the repo, because a three-hour spike window contains mostly untouched samples that were being scored against a fault that never reached them.

Real vs. generated

The first question any reviewer asks, answered before anything else. Station locations are real IMD sites. The readings are generated. They will stay generated through the finals — what is being demonstrated is the pipeline that judges them.

Every accuracy figure in this document rests on faults we injected ourselves. IMD publishes station locations, not a labelled fault list, so there is no real archive to validate against. What injected truth buys instead is exactness: recall and precision here are computed against a ground truth known by construction, not estimated.

16

How accuracy is measured

Every accuracy claim in this project is one of the definitions below. They are not interchangeable, and quoting the wrong one is the easiest way to sound better than you are.

Precision and recall

recall = faults found / faults present did we miss anything? precision = true alarms / all alarms is the queue worth working?
Worked — the actual network result
Stations with an injected fault28
Stations the detector alerted on42
Of those, genuinely faulty (0.38 · 42)16
recall = 16 / 280.57
precision = 16 / 420.38

Read together: of 28 real faults we find 16, and to get those 16 an engineer is asked to look at 42 stations. Twenty-six of those visits find nothing. That is the real cost, and it is the number a maintenance planner cares about far more than recall.

Event recall vs. point recall

These answer different questions and usually differ a lot.

MeasureCountsTells you
Point recallindividual flagged samples inside faultswhether you catch the bulk of a fault
Event recallan event is a hit if any sample in it is flaggedwhether you catch the onset at all

High event recall with low point recall means the system notices faults but does not track them; the reverse means it is good at long obvious faults and misses short ones. The gap between the two is the diagnostic.

The operating point is a choice, not a result

This same detector reaches 86% recall at 15% precision if the bands are loosened. Neither setting is "the accuracy". We publish 57/38 because a queue nobody trusts is worse than a shorter one — but the trade is a dial, and an operator with more field crews should turn it.

18

Explainability, as built

The honest headline

There is no machine-learned model in the decision path. The three agents are deterministic evaluators, the adjudicator is a fixed rule order, and nothing trains or updates online. Calling the panel “agents” describes the architecture — independent specialists, one arbiter, every verdict inspectable — not an LLM in a loop.

Why not SHAP

The problem statement names SHAP/LIME as preferable. Both attribute a model's prediction to its features; applied to a fixed threshold they return the threshold. But SHAP is an approximation of the Shapley value from cooperative game theory, and it approximates only because a model has hundreds of features and 2n coalitions is impossible to enumerate.

We have three agents. 23 is eight. Every coalition is evaluated, so this is the exact Shapley value — the original theorem, not a sampled estimate — for three adjudications more than the verdict itself.

# payout: how hard a coalition pushes toward a visit v(S) = escalation( adjudicate with only the agents in S allowed to speak ) normal → 0.0 warning → 0.5 critical → 1.0 φi = ΣS ( |S|! · (n−|S|−1)! / n! ) · [ v(S ∪ {i}) − v(S) ] weights 1/3, 1/6, 1/3

An absent agent is silenced: it returns unknown with no confidence, which is what the adjudicator already does with an agent that cannot see its evidence, so absence needed no special case. The empty coalition scores zero — all three silent reaches “not enough readings yet” — which makes the contributions sum exactly to the escalation, the efficiency axiom a force plot usually only claims.

Worked — MANDI, relative humidity, from the deployed service
Data quality — reading outside the bandφ = +0.25
Hardware health — nothing to look atφ = 0.00
Weather or fault? — 15 neighbours moved 3.7σφ = −0.25
Sum = escalation as issued → MONITOR0.00

Read the third row: the weather check did not abstain, it argued against dispatch by exactly as much as data quality argued for it, and won. A label saying “data quality carried this” cannot express that; a signed number can.

Each contribution also ships the action the panel would reach without that agent, computed by re-running the adjudicator with it silenced. “Silence hardware and this becomes CALIBRATE” is the half people act on.

Where TreeSHAP would go, and why it is not here

Exact Shapley answers which agent drove the verdict. It cannot answer why the residual was that large, because the reference it is measured against — the median of the neighbours — has no features to attribute to. That is a second layer, and it is where TreeSHAP belongs: once the reference is a gradient-boosted regressor predicting the expected reading from neighbours, hour and season, TreeSHAP decomposes each prediction into per-feature contributions in time linear in the tree. The two attribute different quantities and neither replaces the other — Shapley for the panel, TreeSHAP for the reference.

It is planned and unbuilt. The regressor is not trained and there is no shap import in the repository, so nothing in what runs today should be described as SHAP. The blueprint carries the design; §26 carries the status.

The decision trace

The adjudicator records every check it reached, in order, with the answer it got, shown on the board in a collapsed disclosure. The checks that did not fire carry most of the value: “send someone” means little, while “hardware is fine, the neighbours moved 0.0σ, the readings are 8σ out” is a case. Answers are three-valued — a check whose evidence is missing records n/a, because folding that into “no” reads as a finding when it is an absence.

A limit worth stating before someone else does

The trace walks a fixed decision tree: across all sixty flagged board rows it takes exactly two distinct paths from six question strings. It is an honest printout of the rules and it is not an autonomous investigation — nothing chooses what to examine next based on what it just found. See §26.

Is it really three agents?

Gathering the same Shapley values across all 60 flagged rows answers what no single row can — whether an agent is an ornament.

A finding, and a fix that did not work

Hardware scores zero on all sixty rows because the simulated export carries no housekeeping channels, so it has nothing to read. It fires correctly on the live node, which sends them.

A stuck probe should be visible in the readings, so flat fraction was tried two ways: consecutive unchanged samples (stuck scored 0.24 and 0.50 against a 0.02–0.14 background) and longest unchanged run (stuck scored 0.004, below several healthy stations). Neither separates at this sampling and quantisation, so it is not computed. Lowering the threshold until it fired would be fitting the demonstration rather than detecting anything.

Part IV — As-built
19

Shape of the system

One FastAPI process serves everything: the JSON exports, the live WebSocket, the triage panel, and the compiled React app itself. There is no separate frontend host and no database server.

The panel is the only place the decision rules exist. The board sends the evidence it is allowed to see and renders whatever comes back, holding no judgement of its own — reimplementing “is this a fault” in the browser to save a request is how two screens end up disagreeing about the same sensor.

A stand-in for hardware, not a simulation shortcut. It posts through the same /api/ingest door an ESP32 would use, and it is graded online, against real neighbours, by the same sigma bands the map uses.

Behaviour worth knowing

Pause and resume continue the record rather than restarting it, because a station that stops transmitting and starts again does not begin its record over. Changing Speed retunes the running node in place via /api/live/rate — it does not restart it, which would reset the clock to day 0.

21

Ingest and storage

POST /api/ingest is the single door into the system. It applies the WMO-No. 8 Annex 1.A physics screen (physics/wmo_limits.py), then writes the reading to SQLite in WAL mode via api/store.py. Readings survive a restart; /api/ingest/history and /api/ingest/recent read them back.

It screens but does not reject. physics_screen() is recomputed server-side rather than trusted — a node with a corrupted screen is one of the faults being looked for — and where the node's flags and the server's disagree, edge_screen_disagreement is added to the record. Every reading is stored with whatever flags it earned. This matters for the demo's credibility: in the real world the data is stored, so it is stored here too.

The simulated network does not come through here. It is a pre-built export the panel reads directly, so nothing about it is ingested or stored — the two sources meet at the triage panel rather than at the door. Making the simulation travel the same path is deferred work, listed in §26.

Frontend

React 19 + Vite + TypeScript, TanStack Query for server state, react-router for routing, Leaflet for the map. Compiled to web/dist and mounted by FastAPI at /app. Four routes, and no console — the old HTML console was retired.

Styling is Tailwind v4 via @tailwindcss/vite, with shadcn-idiom primitives (cva + cn). The migration turned on one detail worth recording: the pre-existing stylesheets are imported into an explicitly declared @layer legacy that is listed first, so Tailwind utilities outrank them. Without that declaration unlayered legacy CSS wins every tie by specificity, converted pages render as though nothing changed, and bg-ink text-paper comes out black on black.

web/src/ routes/ HomeRoute · NetworkRoute · BoardRoute · TeamRoute lib/ useLive (WebSocket singleton) · alerts (episodes) · triage (panel client) sticky (sessionStorage state) · bands · field · sim · theme · title · cn api/ client · queries · types · mapTypes · sites components/ AgentPanel · PanelAttribution · DecisionTrace · PanelBehaviour RegionCompare · ExplainMath · StationChannels · BeliefChart Callout · Field · Badge · ThemeToggle · Async components/home/ SkyPanel · XaiLoop · EdgeTiers · DriftFigure · Reveal

The explainability surface is four components and they are deliberately separate: AgentPanel shows the three opinions, PanelAttribution the exact Shapley values as a waterfall with a lane per agent, DecisionTrace the adjudicator's walk in a collapsed disclosure, and PanelBehaviour the same attribution gathered across the whole network. RegionCompare draws the station against its neighbours, which is the evidence the verdict rests on.

lib/sticky.ts keeps the network page's eleven controls in sessionStorage, not localStorage, on purpose: these are working settings that should survive navigating to the board and back but should not still be there tomorrow showing a colleague a view they never chose.

lib/useLive.ts is the other file worth reading before touching anything live: a module-level socket shared by every subscriber, with reconnect and backoff, holding byFrame, grades, frame, pass, running and fault. The dashboard clock is derived from live.frame — if readings stop arriving, time stops on the page. That coupling is deliberate, and it is also why several past bugs presented as "the Play button is broken".

Two things that will confuse the next person

ZeroGPU on a CPU-only project. A free account cannot create a Docker or cpu-basic Space — the API answers 402. ZeroGPU is the only free way to get a Python process, using the documented no-op @spaces.GPU pattern with all real work outside it. No GPU is ever requested and no quota is consumed.

One line holds the whole API up. The entrypoint launches Gradio with prevent_thread_lock=True, then demo.app.mount("/", api). Delete that line and every API route 404s while the Space still reports RUNNING.

Not built

Stated plainly, so nobody rediscovers these as surprises.

GapStateDetail
An autonomous explanation trajectorynot builtThe largest gap. The decision trace is a walk through a fixed tree — two distinct paths across all sixty board rows, from six question strings. A trajectory chooses the next probe because of the previous answer, so different sensors produce genuinely different investigations. The probes largely exist; the controller that selects among them and the stopping rule do not.
A learned reference model, and TreeSHAP over itnot builtThe place a model belongs is predicting what a station should have read from its neighbours, the hour and the season, so the anomaly is the subtraction r = observed − predicted and the model's own error sets the scale. Make that reference a gradient-boosted regressor and TreeSHAP applies exactly where it is meant to — per-feature contributions to each prediction, so a verdict can name which neighbour and which part of the daily cycle the expected value rested on. Designed, not trained; there is no shap import in the repository. Until it exists there is no model in the decision path at all.
Faithfulness testingnot builtNothing verifies the explanations are not lying. We inject the faults, so “does the top-attributed factor match the injected cause?” is measurable here in a way it is not for most entrants. Not yet measured.
One door for both sourcesdeferredAgreed, and not yet done. The live node and a real ESP32 post through /api/ingest and are stored; the simulated network does neither. It is a pre-built export the panel reads directly, and store.record() is called only from api/ingest.py. Routing the simulation through the same door is the correct shape — one path, one screen, nothing special-cased — and the blueprint's system diagram already draws it that way. Until then the shared door is the panel, not ingest: both sources are judged by the same three agents, which is what actually stops two screens disagreeing about one sensor.
Real hardwarenot builtThe firmware runs end to end in Wokwi against the deployed Space. It has never run on a physical ESP32.
Housekeeping on simulated rowsnot builtThe export carries no supply, logger or link channels, so the hardware agent is silent across the whole simulated board. See §18 — deriving it from the readings was attempted and does not separate.
Authenticationnot builtEvery endpoint is open, including the control POSTs.
Multi-node live tiernot builtExactly one live node exists; _state is a single global.
TimescaleDB / Postgresnot builtThe blueprint proposes it; storage today is one SQLite file.
Corrected-value outputnot builtListed as optional in the problem statement; not attempted.
Automated testsnot builtVerification is manual, through the browser and the API.

Traps already paid for

Each of these cost real debugging time. They are recorded so they cost it once.

TrapWhat actually happens
Differencing raw valuesFlags 49% of healthy stations. Remove each station's hourly climatology first (§06).
Sigma fitted in-sampleInflated 1.61×, 7.6% false alarms. It must be a trailing, lagged rolling MAD (§08).
Skipping the MSL reduction100 m of elevation is ~11.6 hPa of pure artefact. Every hill station looks broken.
Magnus water coefficients below 0 °CManufactures supersaturation at cold and high stations. Select the coefficient set by temperature.
Marking a whole spike window as touchedDepresses every spike metric: most samples in the window were never displaced.
Mounted ASGI apps get no lifespan eventsStartup hooks on the mounted FastAPI app never fire on the Space.
Awaiting inside a finally during cancellationThe await is cancelled immediately, so a "publish on shutdown" placed there never leaves the process.
The boundary GeoJSON is a bare FeatureNot a FeatureCollection — clipping is skipped silently if you assume otherwise.
A frame high-water markMath.max made the clock a ratchet: after a restart it could never go back, and froze permanently.
pkill on WindowsDoes nothing. The old server keeps serving stale code while a second one fails to bind. Kill the port owner found via netstat -ano.
Unlayered legacy CSS beats TailwindUtilities lose every tie to un-layered stylesheets. Declare @layer legacy, theme, base, components, utilities first and import the old CSS into legacy, or converted pages render unconverted.
Gating an entrance animation on opacityA paused animation still applies its current keyframe, so a stalled or never-started entrance leaves content at opacity 0 permanently. Five sections shipped invisible. Animate transform only — the worst case is then content fourteen pixels low.
Wokwi has no BME280The part does not exist in its library, so the I²C bus is simply empty and the node looks mute. Use DHT22 behind a #define. Its serial pins are TX/RX, not esp:TX0/esp:RX0, and a slide switch must ground pin 3 — grounding pin 1 boots with the link already cut.
Counting the wrong bitschecks_passed counted zero bits in the self-test mask and reported 1 when all 12 passed. It is 12 − popcount(mask).
Handing an agent its own conclusionAsserting neighbours_agree: false on every flagged row made the context agent's Shapley value structurally zero. An agent that cannot change any outcome is an ornament, and only a global view reveals it.
Attribution ties on a vetoed rowPicking the carrier by largest |φ| credits the agent that lost: +0.25 and −0.25 tie, and the verdict was “no visit”. The carrier is the agent whose absence would change the action.
Overlapping segments on one trackDrawn on a shared axis, two agents who disagree overlap and the one painted last hides the other — erasing exactly the case worth seeing. A lane per agent.
A strict > on a shaded windowAn episode shorter than one chart point collapses to zero width and never draws, on precisely the rows whose window matters most.
